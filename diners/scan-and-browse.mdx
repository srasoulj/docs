You don't install anything to use a Rumizi menu. If the restaurant has a
QR code on the table, your phone already has everything it needs.

## Open the menu

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your camera">
    Use the built-in camera app on iPhone or Android. No special QR
    scanner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point it at the QR code">
    Hold the camera over the code for a second. A link appears at the
    top (on iPhone) or as a notification (on Android).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap the link">
    The venue's page opens in your browser. That's the whole setup.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
Can't scan? Ask your server for the venue's Rumizi link. It's just
`rumizi.com/venue-name`.
</Tip>

## What opens first: the chat

At most venues, scanning drops you into a **chat** rather than a wall of
dishes. It's the [menu assistant](/diners/menu-assistant) — it knows the
menu in front of you, and asking it *"what's good here?"* is usually faster
than scrolling.

The full browsable menu is always one tap away, from the menu button in the
header or at `rumizi.com/venue-name/menu` directly.

<Note>
Some venues turn the chat off. If yours has, scanning takes you straight to
the browsable menu instead and everything below still applies.
</Note>

## A couple of quick taps first

The first time you open a venue's chat, it asks a short set of questions so
it can point you at the right things. Each one is a row of tappable pills:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Is this your first time with us?">
    **First time here** or **I've been before**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Where are you visiting from?">
    **I'm local**, or a region — European, Middle Eastern, South Asian,
    East Asian, Southeast Asian, Latin American, North American, African.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Whereabouts exactly?">
    A country, when the region you picked has a list. Skipped otherwise.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Which language would you like to chat in?">
    The assistant replies in whatever you pick from here on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Progress dots show where you are ("Step 2 of 4"). You can tap **Back** to
change an answer, **Skip** a single question, or **Skip — just ask me
anything** to leave the whole thing and start typing.

<Tip>
It's asked once. Your answers stay on your phone, and the questions don't
come back on your next visit to that venue — you get a "welcome back"
instead.
</Tip>

## Find what you want

The browsable menu is organised in categories — *Starters*, *Mains*,
*Drinks*, and so on. A few things that help:

- **The Popular row at the top.** Venues pin their best dishes here. It
  shows once there are at least three of them.
- **Search.** The search box matches item names, descriptions, ingredients,
  and allergens — so searching *"walnut"* finds the dish whose description
  mentions walnuts, not just one with it in the title.
- **Category jump.** Use the category bar to skip to a section.
- **Switch language** from the header if the default isn't yours. See
  [Languages](/diners/languages).
- **Switch to dark mode** with the theme toggle in the header.

Tags like *Vegetarian* or *Spicy* appear as small coloured labels on the
dishes that have them — up to three per card, then a "+2". They're labels,
not filters; to narrow things down, use search or just ask the assistant.

## Item detail

Tap any dish to open its detail page. You'll see:

- A photo (if the venue added one).
- The full description and price.
- Any allergen or dietary tags.
- **Tappable ingredients.** A row of ingredient chips sits under the
  description. Tap one for a short explanation and two AI-drawn pictures
  of what it actually is — useful when you're not sure what "burrata",
  "guanciale", or "sumac" means. The popup title stays in the menu's own
  wording (what you'd say to your server); the explanation is in your
  language.
- **Goes well with** — the venue's own pairing suggestions, grouped by kind
  so starters, drinks, and desserts are easy to tell apart.
- **Question chips** — three or four ready-made questions built from that
  dish: *"What's in it?"*, *"Without garlic?"*, *"Contains nuts?"*, *"What
  goes with it?"*. Tap one and the answer arrives in the chat.

## "Not for me"

If a dish isn't your thing, tap the **Not for me** control on it. Rumizi
quietly shows you fewer dishes like it — it drops out of the Popular row and
the assistant stops recommending it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap Not for me">
    You'll see *"Got it — we'll quietly show fewer like this"* with an
    **Undo** if you tapped it by accident.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say how far it goes">
    **Just at this restaurant**, or **I don't like this anywhere** — which
    carries the preference to other Rumizi venues too.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say why, if you like">
    An optional row of reasons. **Skip** is always there.
  </Step>
</Steps>

This is anonymous. The venue sees counts of what diners marked, never who
marked it.

## Back button closes overlays

Your phone's Back button (or the swipe-back gesture) closes overlays one
at a time — an ingredient popup, the item detail, the category dialog,
the chat panel — instead of leaving the venue's page. Tap it again to keep
peeling layers back until you're at the menu.

## Out of stock items

If a dish is dimmed and marked "Unavailable", the kitchen is out.
Tapping it still shows the detail page so you can see what it is.

## Ordering

Rumizi's menu is **browse-and-ask** — you use it to decide, then order
with your server the way you normally would. There's no cart, no
"send order" button, no digital ordering. Once you've picked what you
want, put the phone down and tell your server (or hold up the item on
your screen).
